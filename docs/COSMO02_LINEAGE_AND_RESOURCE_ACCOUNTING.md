## Research motivation

The posted illustrations suggested that interactions between "universes" in a "bulk" generate a new expanding universe through black-hole-like filaments. COSMO-01 could study how a coarse observer misses hidden predictive differences in small graphs, but it did not distinguish a new graph vertex/container from new material or physical spacetime.

COSMO-02 addresses the **computational analogy only**: a new child domain can become addressable even though **no new source token or signal bit is created**.

## Fixed experiment contract

**Initial state:** two bounded parent domains, A and B, each with four labeled 0/1-signal tokens (eight unique tokens total). All 256 binary configurations have uniform prior.

**Actions:** (a) hold, (b) local rotation/transform, (c) authorized endpoint swap/transfer, (d) explicitly operator-approved child birth by rehoming the A-last and B-first tokens into a 2-token child with \`parentIds=[A,B]\`. A subsequent, separately authorized merge can retire the child and restore its tokens to their origin parents. The ancestry event remains.

**Invariants:** unique token inventory of eight and sum of initial 1-valued signals unchanged for these exact permutation operations. A failed authorization must not change any token arrangement.

**Keyhole:** observe only parent 1-counts. Compare uncertainty of the child's inherited 1-count using either those coarse readings, those readings plus A-last, or those readings plus both donor bits. With both donors observed, the child's 1-count is defined and predictable without uncertainty. This is not physical entropy, mass or information creation.

**Control:** an attempt to create a child without explicit operator approval and fixture enablement must refuse. The same is true of contact, compatibility or permission gate closure. Clone-like duplicated tokens are intentionally **invalid** and must trigger the audit; any appearance of an added token would be a failed resource ledger.
